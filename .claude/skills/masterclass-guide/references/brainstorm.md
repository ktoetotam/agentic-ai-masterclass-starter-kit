# Brainstorm a build

Use these in the Choose mode. Ask one question at a time and wait for the answer.

## First three questions

1. **Role:** What is your job, and who do you work for or with?
2. **The task:** Which task repeats every week or month, or eats your Friday afternoon?
3. **The material:** What does that task start from (emails, documents, a spreadsheet, websites, notes), and are you allowed to use it with this AI service today?

## More questions if they are unsure

- What do you copy and paste between tools?
- Which report or overview do you rebuild every week or month?
- What do colleagues keep asking you that is written down somewhere?
- Where do you search for something you know you saw before?
- What would you ask a developer to build if one worked for you for a week?
- What would you be proud to show your team on Monday?

## Fit test

Ask these five questions. Count the yes answers.

1. Can a small version work on sample data within one day?
2. Is the input available today: your own approved material, or the fictional sample?
3. Can a person check whether the output is right?
4. Can it work without really sending, paying or changing a live system?
5. Would someone on your team use it next month?

**4–5 yes:** go. **2–3:** make it smaller or start from the sample data. **0–1:** pick a different build.

## Data permission

Before using real material, ask: "Are you allowed to send this to OpenAI or Anthropic under your company's rules?" A local folder does not keep the text local; the AI service reads what the agent sends it. If they are unsure, start with the fictional sample and swap in real material once someone has confirmed it is allowed. Never ask them to paste passwords, bank details or personal data of other people into the chat.

## Ambition ladder

The masterclass asks groups to push agents as hard as possible and to learn where they fail. Climb one step at a time:

1. **Running (by 12:30):** a small version works on the sample data.
2. **Real (by 15:00):** your input, the features that matter, deployed if the build needs a link.
3. **Ambitious (15:30–16:30):** the version you assumed would cost thousands of euros and many developer hours. Pick one big feature from the challenge card's "push further" list.
4. **Known limits (at the demo):** what failed, how you caught it, how much human checking it needs, and what would have to change for real use.

Never skip step 1 to reach step 3.

## Merge group ideas

1. List each person's idea in one line.
2. Look for a shared input or output, for example several ideas that all start from documents.
3. Choose the idea with the clearest demo moment and data the group can use today.
4. Turn the other ideas into "push further" features or into a second input for the same tool.
5. Record the choice and the reason under Decisions in PLAN.md.

## Red flags

Steer away from, or shrink, builds that need:

- logins to company systems that cannot be connected today;
- real customer, patient or employee data without approval;
- legal, medical or financial decisions without a human review step;
- real emails, payments or posts going out;
- paid APIs without an agreed budget;
- "fully automatic, no checking".
